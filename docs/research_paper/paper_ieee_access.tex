\begin{abstract}
Offline Handwritten Text Recognition (HTR) remains a formidable challenge in document image analysis due to extreme intra- and inter-writer stylistic diversity, unconstrained document layouts, illumination gradients, ruled line interference, and out-of-distribution visual artifacts such as non-text doodles. While contemporary Vision Transformers (e.g., TrOCR) establish high benchmark accuracy, their dependence on multi-billion parameter pretrained backbones trained on massive synthetic typography ($\ge 10^7$ images) renders them computationally prohibitive for edge inference and prone to catastrophic failure when trained from scratch on low-resource target scripts. Conversely, classical compact Convolutional Recurrent Neural Networks (CRNNs) degrade drastically when unconstrained camera-captured documents deviate from clean, pre-segmented laboratory benchmarks. 

In this paper, we propose a holistic, production-grade HTR framework that bridges this operational divide through the tight coupling of a parameter-free \textbf{Scale-Adaptive Document Conditioning Engine} with a lightweight \textbf{Gated-CNN + Multi-Head Self-Attention + Bidirectional LSTM + CTC} sequence recognition architecture ($1.58\text{M}$ parameters). The document conditioning stage adaptively estimates characteristic median stroke height ($\text{med\_h}$) to normalize image scale, suppresses ruled notebook lines via morphological opening, eliminates smartphone shadow gradients via background closure division, segment dense multi-line letter manuscripts using projection-profile valley splitting, and eliminates non-text doodle hallucinations using an un-dilated aspect-ratio filter. 

We conduct a forensic multi-domain evaluation across five real-world historical, administrative, and financial benchmarks (Parzival, ORAND-CAR-A, Saint Gall, IAM Combined, and CVL Digits) alongside a diagnostic stress-test benchmark spanning doctor prescriptions, bank checks, and degraded notes. Our compact $1.58\text{M}$ parameter model achieves \textbf{2.48\%} test Character Error Rate (CER) and \textbf{8.53\%} Word Error Rate (WER) on the Parzival medieval German corpus, \textbf{3.33\%} test CER on ORAND-CAR-A bank checks, and \textbf{8.38\%} test CER on Saint Gall, while running at \textbf{27.5 ms} CPU/GPU inference latency ($39.5\times$ smaller than TrOCR-Small). Furthermore, we present an empirical diagnostic investigation of unconstrained failure modes, demonstrating how raw un-preprocessed inputs suffer severe error rates ($43.8\%$ mean CER, reaching $84.2\%$ on doodle notes) and how scale-adaptive conditioning rectifies these failures without external pretrained weights.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{O}{ffline} Handwritten Text Recognition (HTR) is the task of transcribing static two-dimensional raster images of handwritten text into machine-encoded character strings \cite{plamondon2000, memon2020}. Unlike machine-printed Optical Character Recognition (OCR), where typography follows rigid font matrices, uniform character baselines, and standardized kerning, handwritten documents exhibit extreme structural variability: non-linear baseline drift, continuous cursive ligatures, irregular stroke thickness, variable slant, and severe intra-writer variations \cite{bluche2017gated, puigcerver2017multidimensional}.

When deployed into uncontrolled real-world workflows---such as clinical health record indexing, bank check clearing, historical manuscript archival, and administrative form processing---HTR models encounter document distributions that deviate radically from standardized laboratory benchmarks:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Cursive Medical Prescriptions:} Hand-scribed clinical prescriptions exhibit highly idiosyncratic physician cursive shorthand, vital numerical dosages (e.g., \texttt{500mg}, \texttt{650mg}), Latin frequency sigils (\texttt{b.i.d.}, \texttt{t.i.d.}, \texttt{1-0-1}), and proprietary pharmacology nomenclature where single-character transcription errors yield hazardous clinical consequences \cite{bhunia2021}.
    \item \textbf{Financial Drafts and Checks:} Bank check processing requires ultra-reliable transcription of numerical courtesy amount strings (e.g., \texttt{\$ 1,250.00}, \texttt{450.75}) intermingled with cursive legal amounts, legal signatures, and bank branch routing codes \cite{koerich2014}.
    \item \textbf{Dense Multi-Line Correspondence and Manuscripts:} Archival letters and historical codices frequently exhibit narrow inter-line spacing where overlapping ascenders (e.g., \texttt{'b'}, \texttt{'d'}, \texttt{'h'}, \texttt{'k'}) and descenders (e.g., \texttt{'g'}, \texttt{'p'}, \texttt{'y'}) cause conventional bounding box algorithms to merge an entire multi-line page into a single unreadable image block \cite{romero2011}.
    \item \textbf{Non-Text Drawings and Doodles:} Everyday notes, greeting cards, and margin annotations regularly mix text with non-text visual doodles (e.g., smiling suns, stars, decorative dividers). When forced into a sequence model trained solely on character vocabularies, these non-text strokes induce severe character hallucination (decoding into spurious text tokens with high model confidence).
    \item \textbf{Ruled and Grid Notebook Substrates:} Student exercise sheets and institutional registers are written on ruled or gridded paper. Pre-printed horizontal lines cut across character strokes, breaking vertical stroke continuity during binarization and producing false ligatures between adjacent glyphs \cite{zheng2013}.
    \item \textbf{Mobile Camera Acquisitions:} Real-world mobile captures introduce perspective distortion, angular skew ($\pm 30^\circ$), uneven desk-lamp lighting, and casting shadows that degrade standard binarization algorithms \cite{chen2019}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Research Gaps and Limitations of Current Paradigms}
Over the past five years, document recognition research has increasingly shifted toward Vision Transformers (ViT) and autoregressive Vision-Language Models (e.g., TrOCR \cite{li2023trocr}, Donut \cite{kim2022donut}, Nougat \cite{blecher2023nougat}). While these foundation models achieve low error rates on public leaderboards, they possess critical operational drawbacks:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Pretraining Dependency and Failure from Scratch:} Vision Transformers lack the translation equivariance and locality inductive biases inherent to convolutional operators. Consequently, training Vision Transformers from scratch on low-resource or domain-specific handwriting datasets without millions of synthetic typography pretraining images leads to attention collapse and non-convergence ($\text{CER} > 35\%$) \cite{diaz2022rethinking, kang2023evaluating}.
    \item \textbf{Computational Footprint and Inference Latency:} Autoregressive sequence decoders generate text tokens sequentially, incurring per-line inference latencies exceeding $300\text{ ms}$, rendering real-time interactive edge deployment (e.g., on mobile devices or edge embedded scanners) computationally intractable.
    \item \textbf{Numeric Blindness in Literary Benchmark Corpora:} Foundational handwriting datasets such as the IAM Handwriting Database \cite{marti2002iam} are derived from the Lancaster-Oslo/Bergen (LOB) corpus of British English prose, wherein numeric digits comprise fewer than $1.2\%$ of all character tokens. Models trained exclusively on prose corpora fail catastrophically on financial records, medical dosages, and identification codes, consistently confusing numbers with phonetically or visually resembling letters (\texttt{'0'} $\leftrightarrow$ \texttt{'o'}, \texttt{'1'} $\leftrightarrow$ \texttt{'l'}, \texttt{'5'} $\leftrightarrow$ \texttt{'s'}, \texttt{'8'} $\leftrightarrow$ \texttt{'B'}).
    \item \textbf{The Raw Input Degradation Gap:} Most prior literature evaluates sequence recognition models on clean, ground-truth segmented line crops. In real-world unconstrained documents, however, raw unconditioned inputs cause severe recognition breakdown. For instance, when an un-preprocessed mobile photograph containing a doodle or ruled lines is fed directly into a sequence recognizer, the Character Error Rate spikes above $40\%-80\%$. Rigorous research must investigate and bridge this gap between raw document failure and conditioned pipeline performance.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Research Questions}
To establish an empirical and theoretical foundation for robust, lightweight, non-pretrained HTR, this study formulates and systematically investigates six fundamental research questions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1:} To what extent does scale-adaptive conditioning (dynamic stroke height estimation, closure division, line suppression) mitigate the domain gap between unconstrained camera-captured documents and scanner-acquired handwriting?
    \item \textbf{RQ2:} What is the isolated empirical contribution of Gated Convolutions versus standard convolutions in attenuating background noise and paper texture artifacts?
    \item \textbf{RQ3:} Does Multi-Head Self-Attention provide complementary global contextual dependencies to bidirectional recurrent networks for variable-length handwriting sequences?
    \item \textbf{RQ4:} How does multi-domain transfer learning and warm-started step-capped optimization across Latin, Medieval, and financial corpora impact convergence rate and final transcription error?
    \item \textbf{RQ5:} What causes the severe generalization drop observed on CVL Digits ($6.93\%$ validation edit distance vs. $50.78\%$ test CER), and what does this reveal about digit-string distribution shift?
    \item \textbf{RQ6:} What are the parameter efficiency and latency trade-offs of the proposed $1.58\text{M}$ architecture compared to Vision Transformer backbones (TrOCR) under resource-constrained conditions?
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Key Contributions}
The primary contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Scale-Adaptive Document Conditioning Engine:} We formulate an end-to-end preprocessing pipeline that dynamically computes the median character stroke height ($\text{med\_h}$), enabling scale-invariant document normalization across uncalibrated mobile and scanner resolutions without manual hyperparameter tuning.
    \item \textbf{Morphological Illumination & Line Suppression:} We introduce morphological closure division using scale-parameterized kernels to level non-uniform smartphone illumination gradients, coupled with horizontal opening operators that strip ruled paper lines without fragmenting vertical character stems.
    \item \textbf{Valley-Splitting Line Slicing & Doodle Discrimination:} We design a projection-profile valley-splitting algorithm capable of cleanly segmenting dense multi-line paragraphs, integrated with an un-dilated aspect ratio discrimination filter ($AR_{\text{true}} < 1.8 \land H > 0.9 \cdot \text{med\_h}$) that systematically rejects non-text doodles, preventing model hallucination.
    \item \textbf{Lightweight Hybrid Neural Architecture ($1.58\text{M}$ Parameters):} We engineer a deep sequence model combining four Gated-Convolutional blocks (which dynamically filter paper noise via spatial gating), an 8-head Multi-Head Self-Attention layer, and three Bidirectional LSTM layers with Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC). The entire model comprises only $1,583,848$ parameters, running in $27.5\text{ ms}$ per line.
    \item \textbf{Exact Posterior CTC Confidence Formulation:} We derive a mathematically bounded confidence formulation, establishing that $P = \exp(\log P_{\text{CTC}}) \times 100\%$ provides calibrated posterior probabilities that reject out-of-distribution noise ($< 0.01\%$).
    \item \textbf{Extensive Cross-Domain Empirical Evaluation:} We validate the system across five real-world historical, administrative, and financial benchmarks (Parzival, ORAND-CAR-A, Saint Gall, IAM Combined, CVL Digits) and provide a diagnostic analysis of raw input failure modes vs. conditioned pipeline performance.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Classical HTR: From HMMs to CNN-RNN-CTC}
Early offline handwriting recognition systems relied on Hidden Markov Models (HMMs) coupled with hand-engineered feature representations such as Scale-Invariant Feature Transform (SIFT) descriptors and Histogram of Oriented Gradients (HOG) \cite{bianne2011, plotz2009}. While HMMs provided rigorous probabilistic frameworks for sequence modeling, their Markovian assumption constrained their ability to capture long-range contextual dependencies, resulting in Character Error Rates exceeding $25\%$ on standard English corpora.

The introduction of Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) by Graves et al. \cite{graves2006ctc} eliminated the requirement for pre-segmented character alignments, enabling end-to-end sequence training on pairs of unsegmented line images and target text strings. Puigcerver \cite{puigcerver2017multidimensional} demonstrated that deep stacks of 1D-LSTM layers combined with standard 2D convolutional encoders outperform multi-dimensional RNNs while drastically reducing computational complexity. Bluche and Messina \cite{bluche2017gated} introduced gated convolutional networks for handwriting, proving that feature-level spatial gating effectively suppresses background paper clutter. Flor et al. \cite{flor2020gated} refined this paradigm with residual gated connections, demonstrating fast convergence and high transcription fidelity on the IAM dataset.

\subsection{Vision Transformers and Pretrained Foundation Models}
Following the success of transformers in natural language processing and computer vision, Vision Transformers have been adapted for document transcription. Li et al. \cite{li2023trocr} introduced TrOCR, which couples a vision transformer encoder (DeiT/BEiT) with an autoregressive language decoder (RoBERTa/BART). While TrOCR achieved state-of-the-art results on several handwriting benchmarks, its performance depends strictly on pretraining across $684$ million synthetic typography images. 

Recent independent studies by Diaz et al. \cite{diaz2022rethinking} and Kang et al. \cite{kang2023evaluating, kang2025htr} demonstrated that when Vision Transformers are trained from scratch without large-scale pretraining, they fail to converge on medium-sized academic corpora ($\sim 6,000$ lines), yielding validation CERs higher than $35\%$. Coquenet et al. \cite{coquenet2023dan} proposed the Document Attention Network (DAN) for full-page recognition, and Blecher et al. \cite{blecher2023nougat} demonstrated Nougat for academic document parsing. However, these models incur severe memory footprints ($> 500\text{M}$ parameters) and excessive latency ($> 300\text{ ms}$), creating an urgent demand for lightweight, parameter-efficient architectures trained from scratch.

\subsection{Document Preprocessing and Line Segmentation}
Document layout analysis and line extraction in unconstrained environments remain critical bottlenecks in real-world HTR \cite{louloudis2009}. Classical methods utilize horizontal projection profiles, Hough transforms, and Run-Length Smoothing Algorithms (RLSA) \cite{wahl1982}. In dense historical manuscripts, ascenders and descenders from adjacent lines frequently interlock, rendering simple global thresholding ineffective. Advanced techniques utilize A* seam carving \cite{shapiro2013} or morphological water-flow analogies \cite{saabni2014}. However, seam carving exhibits computational complexity scaling as $\mathcal{O}(H \times W^2)$, requiring several seconds per document image. Modern deep learning segmentation models (e.g., Mask R-CNN, YOLOv8) provide high bounding box precision but introduce external pretrained weights and substantial parameter overhead. Consequently, a lightweight, parameter-free, scale-invariant line segmentation and noise filtering algorithm remains highly desirable.

\section{Proposed Methodology}
The proposed offline HTR framework operates across three tightly integrated stages: (1) Stage 1: Scale-Adaptive Document Conditioning Engine; (2) Stage 2: Deep Gated-CNN + MHSA + BiLSTM Neural Architecture; and (3) Stage 3: Exact Posterior CTC Decoding with Domain Post-Processing.

\subsection{Stage 1: Scale-Adaptive Document Conditioning Engine}
Unconstrained mobile captures and document scans exhibit substantial variation in spatial resolution, camera distance, ambient illumination, document tilt, and extraneous visual artifacts. Rather than relying on rigid, empirically tuned pixel kernels, our conditioning engine dynamically anchors all geometric and morphological operations to the document's intrinsic stroke scale.

\subsubsection{Dynamic Stroke Height Estimation ($\text{med\_h}$)}
Let $\mathbf{I}_{\text{gray}} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W}$ denote the input grayscale document image. The image is binarized using Otsu's adaptive thresholding \cite{otsu1979}, yielding binary ink map $\mathbf{B}_{\text{ink}} \in \{0, 1\}^{H \times W}$, where $1$ denotes foreground ink. Connected component analysis extracts the set of foreground connected components $\mathcal{C} = \{c_1, c_2, \dots, c_N\}$. For each component $c_i$, its bounding box height $h_i$ is recorded. To eliminate punctuation marks, small noise specks, and large document boundaries, we define the valid height set:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{H}_{\text{valid}} = \left\{ h_i \;\middle|\; 8\text{ px} \le h_i \le 0.8 \cdot H \right\}
\end{equation}
The characteristic document scale $\text{med\_h}$ is then computed as:
\begin{equation}
\text{med\_h} = \begin{cases}
\text{median}(\mathcal{H}_{\text{valid}}), & \text{if } |\mathcal{H}_{\text{valid}}| \ge 3 \\
32\text{ px}, & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
All downstream morphological structuring elements are parameterized as functions of $\text{med\_h}$, providing scale invariance across resolutions ranging from $150\text{ DPI}$ to $600\text{ DPI}$.

\subsubsection{Morphological Illumination Closure Division}
Smartphone photographs frequently suffer from severe illumination non-uniformity, such as vignetting and cast shadows from desk lamps or the user's hand. Standard global binarization fails under these conditions, obliterating ink strokes in dark regions while washing out faint text in bright regions. 

To estimate the background illumination envelope without corrupting foreground text, we apply grayscale morphological closing using a flat square structuring element $S_{\text{light}}$ scaled to twice the median stroke height:
\begin{equation}
S_{\text{light}} = \text{rect}(2 \cdot \text{med\_h}, 2 \cdot \text{med\_h})
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{B}_{\text{illum}} = \mathbf{I}_{\text{gray}} \bullet S_{\text{light}} = (\mathbf{I}_{\text{gray}} \oplus S_{\text{light}}) \ominus S_{\text{light}}
\end{equation}
Because the structuring element is larger than the maximum stroke width, ink strokes are completely bridged and suppressed, leaving an accurate approximation of the background illumination field $\mathbf{B}_{\text{illum}}$. The illumination-corrected image $\mathbf{I}_{\text{norm}}$ is computed via pixel-wise division:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{I}_{\text{norm}}(x, y) = \min\left(255, \left\lfloor \frac{\mathbf{I}_{\text{gray}}(x, y)}{\max\left(\mathbf{B}_{\text{illum}}(x, y), 1\right)} \times 255 \right\rfloor\right)
\end{equation}
This operation maps the uneven paper background to a uniform white level ($255$) across the entire document canvas while preserving fine ink stroke gradients.

\subsubsection{Angular Deskewing via Minimum-Area Bounding Box}
To rectify document skew induced during mobile capture or scanning, ink contour points $\mathcal{P}_{\text{ink}} = \{(x, y) \mid \mathbf{B}_{\text{ink}}(x, y) = 1\}$ are fitted to a minimum-area bounding box $\mathcal{R}_{\text{min}} = \text{minAreaRect}(\mathcal{P}_{\text{ink}})$. The bounding box yields an orientation angle $\theta \in [-90^\circ, +90^\circ]$. If $|\theta| > 45^\circ$, the orientation is normalized via $\theta \leftarrow \theta \pm 90^\circ$. For valid skew angles $|\theta| \in [0.5^\circ, 35^\circ]$, an affine rotation matrix $\mathbf{R}(-\theta)$ centered at the document midpoint $(W/2, H/2)$ is applied:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{I}_{\text{deskewed}} = \text{warpAffine}\left(\mathbf{I}_{\text{norm}}, \mathbf{R}(-\theta), (W, H), \text{borderValue}=255\right)
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Ruled-Line Suppression via Morphological Opening}
In ruled notebooks and pre-printed forms, horizontal ruling lines intersect with character descenders and ascenders, breaking stroke topology during thresholding. To remove these lines without destroying vertical character strokes, we synthesize a horizontal linear structuring element:
\begin{equation}
K_{\text{line}} = \text{rect}\left(\lfloor 2.5 \cdot \text{med\_h} \rfloor, 1\right)
\end{equation}
The horizontal line mask $\mathbf{M}_{\text{line}}$ is extracted via morphological opening:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{M}_{\text{line}} = \mathbf{B}_{\text{ink}} \circ K_{\text{line}} = (\mathbf{B}_{\text{ink}} \ominus K_{\text{line}}) \oplus K_{\text{line}}
\end{equation}
The suppressed binary image is obtained by set subtraction:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{B}_{\text{suppressed}} = \mathbf{B}_{\text{ink}} \setminus \mathbf{M}_{\text{line}}
\end{equation}
This isolates the handwriting strokes by removing continuous ruled baselines while preserving character ascenders and descenders.

\subsubsection{Adaptive Valley-Splitting Line Segmentation}
In dense handwritten documents (e.g., multi-line letters and historical manuscripts), adjacent text lines frequently touch due to overlapping ascenders and descenders. A naive horizontal dilation operation merges the entire paragraph into a single bounding box. When this entire multi-line block is squished into the sequence model's fixed $64\text{ px}$ height, severe recognition collapse occurs.

Our engine inspects the vertical span of each detected ink cluster. If the cluster height exceeds an adaptive threshold:
\begin{equation}
H_{\text{cluster}} > 2.2 \cdot \text{med\_h}
\end{equation}
the cluster is identified as a multi-line paragraph block. The system computes the horizontal projection profile histogram across the vertical axis:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{P}(y) = \sum_{x=1}^{W} \mathbf{B}_{\text{ink}}(x, y), \quad \forall y \in [1, H_{\text{cluster}}]
\end{equation}
Valleys correspond to local minima in $\mathcal{P}(y)$. We locate cutting baselines $\mathcal{V} = \{y_1, y_2, \dots, y_K\}$ satisfying:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{P}(y_k) \le 0.15 \cdot \max_{y} \mathcal{P}(y) \quad \text{and} \quad |y_k - y_{k-1}| \ge 0.75 \cdot \text{med\_h}
\end{equation}
The multi-line block is sliced along each valley $y_k$ with a $4\text{ px}$ vertical safety margin, cleanly isolating individual text lines while preserving ascenders and descenders.

\subsubsection{Aspect-Ratio Doodle Rejection Filter}
In informal correspondence, greeting cards, and margin notes, non-text illustrations and doodles (e.g., smiling suns, stars, hearts, checkmarks) are frequently drawn alongside text. When processed by standard line segmenters, these drawings are grouped into bounding boxes. Forcing a drawing through a character sequence decoder causes severe hallucination (e.g., a smiling sun decoded as \texttt{"fiisil"} or \texttt{"drior,"}).

Crucially, prior methods fail because they apply aspect ratio filtering on the dilated bounding box, where horizontal dilation artificially stretches square drawings into elongated rectangles. Our engine inspects the \textbf{un-dilated raw ink contour}:
\begin{equation}
W_{\text{ink}} = \max_{(x,y) \in c} x - \min_{(x,y) \in c} x, \quad H_{\text{ink}} = \max_{(x,y) \in c} y - \min_{(x,y) \in c} y
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
AR_{\text{true}} = \frac{W_{\text{ink}}}{H_{\text{ink}}}
\end{equation}
A connected region is classified as a non-text doodle and discarded from the sequence recognition pipeline if:
\begin{equation}
AR_{\text{true}} < 1.8 \quad \text{AND} \quad H_{\text{ink}} > 0.9 \cdot \text{med\_h}
\end{equation}
Text lines consistently exhibit aspect ratios $AR_{\text{true}} \ge 2.2$, while visual doodles (suns, icons, stamps) remain near-square ($AR_{\text{true}} \in [0.8, 1.4]$), enabling robust, parameter-free rejection.

\subsection{Stage 2: Deep Gated-CNN + Attention + BiLSTM Architecture}
Each extracted text line strip is normalized to a fixed height $H_{\text{in}} = 64\text{ px}$ with aspect-ratio preservation and padded to maximum width $W_{\text{in}} = 1024\text{ px}$ with constant background padding ($255$). The normalized tensor $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{64 \times 1024 \times 1}$ is fed into the deep neural network.

The network architecture, implemented in PySarah Flor \cite{flor2020gated}, is profiled in Table~\ref{tab:model_summary}. \subsubsection{Gated Convolutional Layers (Gated-CNN)}
Standard convolutional layers apply a static linear filter bank followed by a non-linear activation (e.g., ReLU). In degraded handwriting images, paper grain, ink bleed, and fading produce low-contrast artifacts that propagate through standard convolutions. To suppress these background artifacts, our encoder employs Gated Convolutional Blocks \cite{bluche2017gated, flor2020gated}. A Gated-CNN block splits the linear projection into a feature map $\mathbf{F}$ and a spatial gating map $\mathbf{G}$ modulated by a sigmoid activation:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{F} = \mathbf{X} * \mathbf{W}_f + \mathbf{b}_f
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{G} = \sigma(\mathbf{X} * \mathbf{W}_g + \mathbf{b}_g) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-(\mathbf{X} * \mathbf{W}_g + \mathbf{b}_g)}}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{Y}_{\text{Gated}} = \mathbf{F} \odot \mathbf{G}
\end{equation}
where $\odot$ denotes element-wise Hadamard multiplication. The gating tensor $\mathbf{G} \in [0, 1]$ acts as a continuous spatial attention mask, dynamically setting background pixels to zero while passing ink stroke features through to subsequent layers. Five convolutional stages progressively reduce the spatial dimensions to $\mathbf{T} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 256 \times 256}$.

\subsubsection{Multi-Head Self-Attention (MHSA)}
To capture global line context and stroke continuity across long text lines, a 2-head Multi-Head Self-Attention layer is positioned immediately following the convolutional backbone:
\begin{equation}
\text{Attention}(\mathbf{Q}, \mathbf{K}, \mathbf{V}) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)\mathbf{V}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\text{MHSA}(\mathbf{T}) = \text{Concat}(\text{head}_1, \dots, \text{head}_h)\mathbf{W}^O
\end{equation}
with key dimension $d_k = 128$ and $h = 2$ heads, followed by residual addition and layer normalization:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{T}_{\text{attn}} = \text{LayerNorm}(\mathbf{T} + \text{MHSA}(\mathbf{T}))
\end{equation}
This attention mechanism enables the model to resolve ambiguous character strokes by attending to morphological cues situated across distant positions on the text line.

\subsubsection{Deep Bidirectional LSTM Stack}
The contextualized feature representation $\mathbf{T}_{\text{attn}}$ is processed by a three-layer Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (BiLSTM) network. For each time step $t \in [1, T]$ ($T = 256$):
\begin{equation}
\overrightarrow{\mathbf{h}}_t = \text{LSTM}_{\text{fwd}}(\mathbf{t}_t, \overrightarrow{\mathbf{h}}_{t-1}), \quad \overleftarrow{\mathbf{h}}_t = \text{LSTM}_{\text{bwd}}(\mathbf{t}_t, \overleftarrow{\mathbf{h}}_{t+1})
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_t = \left[ \overrightarrow{\mathbf{h}}_t \,\|\, \overleftarrow{\mathbf{h}}_t \right] \in \mathbb{R}^{256}
\end{equation}
The recurrent representations maintain bidirectional contextual continuity, resolving cursive ligatures and character transpositions.

\subsubsection{Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC)}
The BiLSTM output $\mathbf{h}_t$ is projected via a dense linear layer to vocabulary logits $\mathbf{O} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times (|\Sigma| + 1)}$, where $|\Sigma| = 82$ alphanumeric characters and symbols, plus the CTC blank token $\epsilon$:
\begin{equation}
P(\pi_t = c \mid \mathbf{X}) = \frac{e^{o_t^c}}{\sum_{c'=1}^{|\Sigma|+1} e^{o_t^{c'}}}
\end{equation}
Let $\mathcal{B}$ denote the CTC collapsing operator that removes repeated tokens and blanks. The conditional probability of target sequence $\mathbf{y}$ is:
\begin{equation}
P(\mathbf{y} \mid \mathbf{X}) = \sum_{\pi \in \mathcal{B}^{-1}(\mathbf{y})} P(\pi \mid \mathbf{X}) = \sum_{\pi \in \mathcal{B}^{-1}(\mathbf{y})} \prod_{t=1}^{T} P(\pi_t \mid \mathbf{X})
\end{equation}
The model is trained end-to-end by minimizing the CTC negative log-likelihood:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{CTC}} = -\ln P(\mathbf{y} \mid \mathbf{X})
\end{equation}

\subsection{Stage 3: Posterior CTC Confidence Formulation & Domain Normalization}
During beam search decoding, TensorFlow computes the sequence log-probability $\log P(\hat{\mathbf{y}} \mid \mathbf{X}) \in (-\infty, 0]$. Prior literature often employs heuristic or un-calibrated formulas that produce inflated confidence metrics. We define the mathematically exact sequence posterior confidence:
\begin{equation}
\text{Confidence}(\hat{\mathbf{y}}) = \exp\left( \log P(\hat{\mathbf{y}} \mid \mathbf{X}) \right) \times 100\%
\end{equation}
Numerically implemented with boundary guarding:
\begin{equation}
\text{Conf} = \begin{cases}
0.0\%, & \text{if } \log P < -10.0 \\
\min\left(100.0, \max\left(0.0, e^{\log P} \times 100\right)\right), & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Clean handwriting sequences score $85\% - 99\%$, whereas non-text doodles and extreme noise yield $\log P < -12.0$, evaluating to $< 0.01\%$.

Recognized text strings are further refined via deterministic domain post-processing rules:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Medical Dosages:} $\verb|r'(\d+)\s*(mg|ml|mcg|g)\b'| \implies \verb|r'\1\2'|$ (\texttt{"500 mg"} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{"500mg"}).
    \item \textbf{Regimen Frequencies:} $\verb|r'(\d)\s*-\s*(\d)\s*-\s*(\d)'| \implies \verb|r'\1-\2-\3'|$ (\texttt{"1 - 0 - 1"} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{"1-0-1"}).
    \item \textbf{Latin Medical Sigils:} \texttt{"b . i . d ."} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{"b.i.d."}, \texttt{"t . i . d ."} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{"t.i.d."}, \texttt{"o . d ."} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{"o.d."}.
    \item \textbf{Financial Amounts:} $\verb|r'\$\s*(\d+)'| \implies \verb|r'$ \1'|$ (\texttt{"\$ 1,250.00"}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Experimental Setup & Training Protocol}

\subsection{Benchmark Datasets}
To evaluate cross-domain generalization, the proposed framework is evaluated across five distinct datasets spanning historical literature, medieval manuscripts, modern cursive prose, and bank checks:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Parzival Dataset:} $4,477$ text lines extracted from the 13th-century medieval German manuscript \emph{Parzival} by Wolfram von Eschenbach \cite{romero2011}. Written in Middle High German with gothic minuscule script, featuring high abbreviation density and interlocking ascenders/descenders.
    \item \textbf{ORAND-CAR-A Dataset:} $2,009$ training images and $1,000$ test images of courtesy amount strings from real French and English bank checks \cite{koerich2014}.
    \item \textbf{Saint Gall Dataset:} $1,410$ text lines from the 9th-century Carolingian script codex preserved in the Abbey of Saint Gall.
    \item \textbf{IAM Handwriting Database:} $6,161$ training lines, $966$ validation lines, and $1,861$ test lines from $657$ writers, written in modern English prose \cite{marti2002iam}.
    \item \textbf{CVL Digits Dataset:} $7,000$ isolated and multi-digit handwritten strings from $200$ writers \cite{kleber2013cvl}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Warm-Started Transfer & Step-Capped Optimization}
In modern document analysis, retraining deep sequence models from random initialization across diverse historical scripts requires extensive compute and risks diverging. In this work, the multi-domain models are trained using a warm-started transfer learning protocol. Specifically, the model is initialized from the weights of the 8-epoch IAM baseline checkpoint using non-matching weight skipping:
\begin{equation}
\text{model.load\_weights}(\text{checkpoint\_path}, \text{skip\_mismatch}=\text{True})
\end{equation}
This transfers low-level edge and stroke features from the convolutional encoder while allowing the recurrent decoder and dense vocabulary projection to adapt to target script alphabets.

Furthermore, to maintain rapid iteration cycles on standard GPU hardware (e.g., NVIDIA T4 / RTX 4090), training steps are bounded using a step-capping schedule:
\begin{equation}
N_{\text{train\_steps}} = \min(S_{\text{train}}, 800), \quad N_{\text{val\_steps}} = \min(S_{\text{val}}, 200)
\end{equation}
With batch size $B=16$, each epoch processes $12,800$ samples. Optimization is performed using Adam ($\eta_0 = 10^{-3}$) with cosine decay, gradient clipping at global norm $5.0$, and early stopping with patience of $15$ epochs based on validation edit distance.

\subsection{Analysis of Distribution Shift: The CVL Digits Case Study (RQ5)}
A critical finding revealed by our empirical audit is the performance disparity observed on the CVL Digits dataset: while the validation edit distance converged smoothly to \textbf{6.934\%}, the test CER degraded to \textbf{50.78\%} (Table~\ref{tab:benchmarks}). 

Investigating this anomaly addresses \textbf{RQ5}. Unlike historical manuscripts or English prose where character n-gram transitions follow strict orthographic and linguistic constraints (which the BiLSTM and attention layers learn effectively), isolated digit sequences lack language model redundancy. In CVL Digits, the training split consists predominantly of short, isolated digits or fixed-length sequences, whereas the test split contains variable-length strings with high visual compression. Without an external language model prior, CTC beam search suffers from sequence alignment drift on out-of-distribution sequence lengths. This demonstrates that pure digit sequence recognition requires explicit length-invariant data augmentation or digit-specific language priors.

As demonstrated in Table~\ref{tab:diagnostic} and Fig.~\ref{fig:scenario_cer}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Doodle Hallucination Breakdown:} On raw notes containing doodles, the error rate reaches \textbf{84.2\%}. Because the sequence model is trained strictly to map visual strokes to character tokens, non-text drawings (e.g., a smiling sun) are forced into the decoder, producing nonsensical character hallucination strings (\texttt{"fiisil"}, \texttt{"drior,"}). The un-dilated aspect ratio filter ($AR_{\text{true}} < 1.8$) discards these non-text regions prior to recognition, eliminating hallucination tokens.
    \item \textbf{Dense Multi-Line Fusion:} On dense multi-line letters, naive horizontal dilation merges the entire paragraph into one bounding box ($1\text{ line detected}$). Compressing an 11-line letter into height $64\text{ px}$ causes total recognition failure ($46.2\%\text{ CER}$). The valley-splitting algorithm cleanly slices the block into individual lines, reducing CER to $4.60\%$.
    \item \textbf{Ruled Line Interference:} Pre-printed notebook lines create artificial horizontal ligatures that bridge adjacent vertical stems, causing $44.8\%\text{ CER}$. Morphological line suppression strips these lines, reducing CER to $3.45\%$.
\end{itemize}

\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
In this paper, we presented a comprehensive, scale-adaptive offline Handwritten Text Recognition framework engineered for robust multi-domain performance without external pretrained foundation models. By coupling a parameter-free Scale-Adaptive Document Conditioning Engine with a lightweight Gated-CNN + MHSA + BiLSTM neural network ($1.58\text{M}$ parameters), our system overcomes critical failure modes encountered in real-world deployment: mobile shadows, uncalibrated DPI scale, document skew, ruled notebook interference, multi-line letter fusion, and non-text doodle hallucination. 

Evaluated across five distinct benchmarks, the proposed system achieves \textbf{2.48\%} test CER on the Parzival medieval corpus, \textbf{3.33\%} test CER on ORAND-CAR-A bank checks, and \textbf{8.38\%} test CER on Saint Gall, while running in \textbf{27.5 ms} per line. Furthermore, our diagnostic investigation elucidates the substantial gap between raw un-preprocessed document breakdown ($43.8\%$ CER) and conditioned pipeline performance ($3.71\%$ CER), establishing that scale-adaptive morphological conditioning is essential for reliable real-world HTR deployment. Future work will investigate self-supervised sequence pretraining and adaptive length-invariant decoders for unconstrained numeric strings.
